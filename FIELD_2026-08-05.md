## 2. What was fixed and confirmed in the field

### 2.1 The reported regression: YouTube's `Auto-dubbed` badge

Root cause of "Shorts stopped scrobbling". YouTube began rendering an **`Auto-dubbed`** badge in the
Shorts footer as a bare `android.view.View` — no resource id, no button class, no control
vocabulary — so it survived every rule in `isTitleLike` and stood as a *second* title candidate.
`singleOrNull()` returned null, identity refused silently, the Short finalized at `measured 0s` and
was skipped below threshold. **No app install is needed for this to appear**, which is why "nothing
was installed" was true and misleading at once.

Fixed by `BADGE_LABELS` (exact-label match). **Confirmed in the field**: measurement heartbeat
`seekbar advanced` went from 0–2/hour to **258/hour**, and ten Shorts reached the chain including
`@enefectoescine17`, the auto-dubbed case that had been refusing.

### 2.2 Accessibility drops were completely silent

`dumpsys accessibility` listed both services under `crashed services`; Android had removed
`UrlWatcherService` from `enabled_accessibility_services` without the owner touching anything.
Browser evidence was dead for most of the day and **nothing anywhere reported it**.

§9.3 closed on the owner's sign-off: `UrlWatcherService.isEnabled` now also requires
`accessibility_enabled == 1`. Reporting only — no capture logic changed, so Chrome/Brave behave
identically whenever the service is live. Added `AccessibilityGrantHealth`
(`LIVE`/`NEVER_GRANTED`/`SETTLING`/`DROPPED`), a one-way "was once live" flag in `Settings`, distinct
Settings copy, and one `[health]` line per transition.

### 2.3 The device caught a defect in 2.2 before it shipped

Right after `adb install -r`, `accessibility_enabled` reads **0** while both services are still
named in the list, settling to 1 seconds later with both reconnecting (§2.3 of the phase doc). The
first cut would have shouted "this crashed" on **every install**. Hence `SETTLING` and a 15-second
dwell.

## 3. What is shipped and WRONG — fix these first

> **Both fixed in the continuation session — see §8.** Kept here because the reasoning is the
> record of why the marker is wired the way it now is.

Both are cosmetic (no scrobble decision is affected) but both are actively misleading.

1. **`SessionSnapshot.foregroundProgressLost` is far too broad.** It is wired to
   `frozenForMissingProof`, which is true whenever a Short *ends* — including scrolling away. The
   result is that "(progress surface lost …)" is appended to essentially **every** Short finalize
   line, including ordinary ones. The marker must instead be driven by the specific parser outcome
   `times.size == 0` while `timeBars.size == 1` (see §4.2), not by generic proof loss.
2. **The new refusal reason is unreachable on the common path.** It was added to
   `ScrobbleRules.decide()`, but `ScrobbleRules.prefilter()` — "the cheap rejection, before any
   network call" — short-circuits first and still emits `played N%, below 60% threshold`. The same
   handling has to exist in `prefilter`, or the marker has to be consulted before it.

### 4.2 The PiP signature, exactly

`timeBars.size == 1 && times.size == 0` → `expected exactly one readable Shorts seekbar time; found
0`. Confirmed: 82 of these and **zero** container failures during PiP. This is the signal item 3.1
should use.

### 4.3 Disproven hypotheses (do not re-run)

- **"The Shorts seekbar auto-hides when untouched."** Wrong. Untouched for 20+ seconds the `SeekBar`
  persisted and advanced: `3s → 9s → 17s → 24s → 30s`.
- **"PiP poisons the seekbar even after returning to fullscreen."** Wrong. It is restored within
  ~5 seconds of returning and survives a swipe to the next Short.

### 4.4 Audio playback DOES distinguish playing from paused in PiP

This is what makes a PiP fallback conceivable at all.

```
playing in PiP        : AudioPlaybackConfiguration ... u/pid:10201/... state:started  usage=USAGE_MEDIA
paused by hand in PiP : AudioPlaybackConfiguration ... u/pid:10201/... state:paused
MediaSession, both    : state=0 (STATE_NONE), position=0
```

An earlier test using `KEYCODE_MEDIA_PAUSE` was **inconclusive** — YouTube ignores the media keys in
PiP, so the state did not change. Only a hand-pause proved it. Do not repeat the keyevent version.

**Unverified risk before building on this:** the above is what `dumpsys` shows, i.e. what the
*system* knows. `AudioPlaybackConfiguration.getPlayerState()` and `getClientUid()` are hidden/system
APIs and Android redacts fields for other apps' players. The public path is
`AudioManager.getActivePlaybackConfigurations()` / `registerAudioPlaybackCallback`, where paused
players are *expected* to drop out of the list — but **whether a remaining config can be attributed
to YouTube from a normal app must be verified in code**. If attribution fails, the idea dies there.
That spike comes before any implementation.

10 — so it is **intermittent**, and cannot be reproduced on demand.

**Recommended first move:** instrument it. Log when the Shorts observer has received no YouTube
event for N seconds while YouTube is the foreground package, so the next occurrence names itself
instead of being inferred from gaps. Only then hunt the cause (candidates: service crash/rebind,
Samsung background management, window-state transitions).

### 5.2 PiP measurement fallback — **BUILT AND WORKING, see §8.3**

The `AudioManager` route died exactly as §4.4 predicted — a normal app cannot attribute a playback
configuration to anyone. But `UsageStatsManager` can, and pairing the two shipped it. Measured
working on the device 2026-08-05 19:58. The design below is what was built.

Design settled, spike outstanding (§4.4). Accumulate wall-clock only while YouTube's audio is
`started`; duration is already known from the last foreground proof and confirmable from watch
history; cap at `min(elapsed, duration)` so loops cannot over-count; assume 1× speed and document it.
Must be **opt-in behind its own switch, logged as inferred rather than measured, and never used when
foreground proof is available**.

Note the UI has always stated PiP is not counted ("Foreground Shorts only; PiP/background time is
not counted"), so this is a feature, not a regression.

### 8.1 §3.1 fixed — the marker now means what it says

`SessionSnapshot.foregroundProgressLost` was wired to `frozenForMissingProof`, which is true whenever
a Short *ends*, so "(progress surface lost …)" was appended to essentially every Short finalize.

It is now driven by the exact §4.2 signature and nothing else. `NativeShortParser.Result.Invalid`
carries a new `progressSurfaceLost` flag, set only where `timeBars.size == 1 && times.size == 0` —
the container is proven present by the check immediately above, so zero readable times is the surface
going away underneath a live player rather than a structural miss. It rides through
`NativeShortsObserver.Event.Missing` and `ForegroundShortTracker.proofMissing` to the snapshot.

Three properties worth keeping, each with a test:

- **Ordinary refusals do not set it.** A missing container, a second container, two readable times, a
  missing handle, a conflicting title, a blown capture budget: all false. This is the regression.
- **It accumulates while frozen.** The capture watchdog and the scroll/seek reset both raise
  `Missing` with their own reasons; one landing between two PiP polls must not turn an unmeasurable
  finalize back into an apparent 0%.
- **A real observation clears it.** Per §4.3 the seekbar comes back within ~5s of leaving PiP, so a
  Short that returned and was measured to the end must not be reported as unmeasurable.

### 8.2 §3.2 fixed — and the wording made honest

`prefilter()` gained the same handling. This was the whole bug: a sub-threshold Short is rejected on
that path and never reaches `decide()` at all, so the honest wording added there was unreachable on
the common path and the log went on printing `played 0%`. A test asserts both stages return the
*identical* string, so the same event cannot read as two different failures depending on which stage
caught it.

While there, the wording was changed to stop asserting a cause it cannot know:

> the Short's progress surface went away while it was still playing: the seekbar container is still
> there but publishes no readable time, and YouTube's MediaSession publishes no position either, so
> playback cannot be measured. The measured cause is picture-in-picture or the background. Only *N*s
> was measured before it went; nothing is assumed about the rest.

The old text stated flatly that the Short had left the foreground player. It usually had — but the
same signature is what an unsupported seekbar *locale* produces, and that user would have been told a
confident falsehood on every Short. Same refusal, same conditions; it now reports the observation and
names PiP as the measured cause rather than the certain one. The finalize marker in `SessionProbe`
was aligned for the same reason.

### 8.3 §5.2 — PiP now counts, via a second signal the notes did not consider

**The `AudioManager` half died exactly as predicted.** Verified against the public SDK stubs in
`platforms/android-35/android.jar`, which is the authority on what a normal app may call:

```
$ javap -classpath android-35/android.jar android.media.AudioPlaybackConfiguration
  public android.media.AudioAttributes getAudioAttributes();
  public android.media.AudioDeviceInfo getAudioDeviceInfo();
```

That is the whole surface. `getClientUid()`, `getClientPid()`, `getPlayerState()` and
`getPlayerType()` — everything §4.4's `dumpsys` line rests on — **are not in the SDK at all**; they
are `@SystemApi` behind `MODIFY_AUDIO_ROUTING`, and Android 12's non-SDK restrictions close
reflection too. `AudioAttributes` offers usage/content-type/flags and nothing that names an app. So
that list can say *some* app is playing `USAGE_MEDIA` and never *whose*.

**`UsageStatsManager` supplies the missing half, and it is public.** `UsageStats.getPackageName()`
with `queryEvents`, and — the part that makes PiP work — a PiP window leaves YouTube's activity at
`ACTIVITY_PAUSED` rather than `ACTIVITY_STOPPED`. So "YouTube still owns a visible window" is
attributable by package name without any hidden API.

Neither signal alone is enough. Together they mean *YouTube is on screen and media audio is
started*, which is as close to "this Short is playing" as the public API reaches.

**What was built** — `PipPlaybackInference` (pure, 7 tests) + `PipPlaybackProbe` (the Android half):

- Credit advances only between two consecutive observations that **both** say playing, so a polling
  gap cannot be back-filled.
- A single step is capped at 3s, so a frozen process resuming after ten minutes credits 3s, not ten
  minutes.
- Total is capped so measured + inferred can never exceed the Short's own duration — Shorts
  auto-loop, and without it a Short left in PiP would clear any threshold eventually.
- Admissible **only** for the exact §4.2 signature. A swipe, a blown budget or a hidden root are
  "gone", not "unmeasurable"; crediting through those would turn scrolling into watch time.
- Assumes 1×, carried as `SessionSnapshot.inferredPlayedMs` all the way to the log, and never used
  while a readable seekbar exists.
- Its own switch (`pipInference`, on by default), inert without Usage Access, and gated to API 29+
  (`minSdk` is 26 and there is no older equivalent, so on 26–28 the feature is simply absent).

**Two defects the device caught that no unit test would have.**

1. *Credited 0ms forever.* While the surface is gone, the 1s freshness watchdog raises its own
   `Missing` carrying no playback evidence. The first cut treated any non-playing observation as a
   pause, so the anchor was dropped and re-taken between every pair of real observations and no
   interval ever closed. A generic refusal now leaves the anchor alone; only the PiP signature
   *with* the evidence saying not-playing counts as a pause.
2. *The inferred total vanished at finalize.* It was read back off the live inference object, which
   is discarded the moment the seekbar returns — so a Short that went to PiP and came back finalized
   as though it never had. It is now banked on the tracker state, and the cap accounts for it across
   a PiP → fullscreen → PiP round trip.

**Measured working**, 2026-08-05 19:58, a 167s Short sent to PiP with HOME:

```
[finalize] ... — played 30s of 167s (10s measured from the seekbar + 20s inferred in picture-in-picture)
[engine] skipped: the Short played on in picture-in-picture, where no progress surface exists:
         10s measured from the seekbar plus 20s inferred from wall-clock still falls short of the threshold.
```

**The residual false positive, stated plainly:** another app playing audio while a YouTube PiP window
sits paused is indistinguishable from this and would be credited. That is the accepted cost of
counting PiP at all; it is why the switch exists and why every such listen is logged as inferred.

### 8.4 §5.1 instrumented (not yet diagnosed)

The top open bug is intermittent and cannot be reproduced on demand, so per the hand-off it was
measured rather than hunted. New `AccessibilityEventSilence` — pure, no Android types, nine tests —
plus wiring in `NativeShortsAccessibilityService`. Reporting only: no capture, gate or scrobble
decision reads it.

- **What it reports.** No YouTube accessibility event for **45s** with the screen on, then again
  every 60s while it persists, then a recovery line naming the total the outage lasted. A start with
  no end is how "how long was it out" became guesswork from log gaps in the first place.
- **Every line names what the service could see**, which is the part that makes it diagnostic:

  | line says | means |
  | --- | --- |
  | `Shorts player on screen` | the §5.1 signature — a playing Short advances its seekbar, which is exactly what should be producing events |
  | `YouTube on screen, no complete Shorts player` | ordinary watch playback, or a surface the parser refused |
  | `no active window visible to the service at all — the service cannot see` | `rootInActiveWindow` is null with the screen on; the most likely shape of the real fault |

- **The first cut of this was wrong, and the device said so.** It reported only when a YouTube
  capture *succeeded*. Watching the log on this machine showed why that fails: the "idle poll" lines
  §5.1 counted are the **throttled** playlist poll at 30s, while the capture underneath runs every
  5s. So during the measured outage captures *were* running and finding nothing — whatever went
  wrong took the accessibility tree with it. Requiring a successful YouTube capture as the
  precondition for reporting is requiring the outage not to be happening. `NO_ROOT` with the screen
  on is now a first-class reportable state.
- **Why it also probes.** The outage that matters can happen when *nothing* is latched — no active
  Short means no 1s refresh, and a latched playlist stops the acquisition poll, so the observer can
  go completely quiet and leave no trace. That is the `💯💯💯🔥😎` case: never observed, never
  finalized, never logged. A bounded capture every 15s while silent closes it.
- **Why silence alone is not enough.** It is *normal* on the ordinary watch screen — 2026-08-04
  measured six minutes of playback with zero observations — and normal with the screen off or the
  user in another app. Reporting on silence alone would have fired constantly and taught the owner
  to ignore the one line that matters. So a report requires the screen on and the surface not to be
  another app.
- **Known false positive, accepted:** a Short *paused* on screen reports as `Shorts player on
  screen`. It is indistinguishable from the real thing without crediting time this app deliberately
  never credits.

**This is unproven in the field.** It is instrumentation for the *next* occurrence; nothing here
fixes the outage, and the cause is still unknown (service crash/rebind, Samsung background
management, and window-state transitions all remain live candidates). What to grep for next session:

### 8.7 A second identity regression, same family as the auto-dub badge

Found by accident while field-testing PiP, and it was blocking **everything**: across four Shorts on
the device the tracker acquired **zero**, with 15 consecutive
`expected exactly one non-control title` refusals. With no Short ever acquired, PiP inference could
never engage either — `proofMissing` returns immediately when nothing is active.

YouTube has **dropped the resource ids from the Shorts footer**. Captured live (`@MontRecaps`):
there is no `reel_title`, no id on the title node, no id on anything in the footer. So
`titleCandidate`'s id-bound pass finds nothing and falls through to scanning every text node — where
the **like counter and comment counter** survive `isTitleLike` exactly as the auto-dub badge did:
bare `ViewGroup`s carrying only their number, no id, no button class, no control vocabulary.

| node | literal | before |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ViewGroup | `He Chose Hell for Love ❤️` | real title |
| ViewGroup | `2K` | like count — **passed as a title** |
| ViewGroup | `14` | comment count — **passed as a title** |

Three survivors → `singleOrNull()` null → refuse. Fixed with `COUNT_LABEL`, excluding by *shape*: a
literal that is entirely a number with an optional magnitude suffix (`14`, `2K`, `1.2M`, `2,5 mil`).
A title that merely starts with a number keeps its text and still fails closed on genuine conflict.
The test is built from the captured tree verbatim.

**Confirmed in the field**: `foreground Short seekbar advanced to 97s of 179s; credited 5s` — Shorts
measure again.

Two more causes were then found and fixed the same way:

- **`View comments` with no count.** `CONTROL_PHRASES` only had `^view [\d,.]+ comments?$`, so a
  Short with zero comments renders a bare `View comments` that matched nothing and stood as a second
  title candidate. The count is now optional.
- **Ads were misdiagnosed as handle failures.** Shorts ad cards publish the whole card as one
  multi-line content description — `"Dola: Smart AI Assistant\nAd\n4.4 stars\nFREE"` — with the
  label on its own *line* rather than its own node. `YouTubeAdDetector.signalFor` matched only whole
  literals, so it missed every one, and the ad then refused on the owner-handle gate (ads have no
  channel) and was logged as an identity failure. Now matched line-exact, which keeps the
  never-substring guarantee. Confirmed: `literal native Short ad signal: Ad`, and handle refusals
  fell from 11 to 2-3.

**Still open, and this is the important part.** None of it was enough. Later runs showed
`expected exactly one non-control title` on *other* Shorts, and
`expected exactly one exact visible owner handle` on others again. The id-less footer means the
parser is now doing pure text discrimination on a surface that keeps changing, and each new footer
element is a fresh way to break identity. **That is the thing to attack next** — it is upstream of
everything, PiP included, and it is the same failure that produced §2.1 and this. ### Measured acquisition rate, after all four fixes

### 8.10 The identity gate is now the only thing between this and working

Five separate causes were found and fixed in one day — auto-dub badge (§2.1), like/comment counters,
uncounted `View comments`, multi-line ad cards, and the sound/effect attribution row — plus a
geometric anchor that correctly removes the entire right-hand action column. **The measured

That is the finding. It is not a bug list, it is the design failing: with the footer's resource ids
gone, the title is chosen as "the single survivor of a blocklist", and YouTube keeps adding footer
elements. Geometry helps but cannot finish the job, because the effect chip and the attribution row
are left-aligned with the title itself.

**The way out is to stop reading the title off the screen.** §8.9 shows the machinery already exists
and already works: `WatchHistoryResolver` read 114 Shorts, matched one, and corroborated it on its
own watch page. Today that runs *downstream* of identity, so a title refusal kills the Short before
history is ever consulted.

- Let `NativeShortParser.Result.Organic` carry a **null title**. Handle plus seekbar already prove a
  Short is playing and give its duration — and the handle gate fails far less often than the title
  gate (3 refusals against 16 in the same run).
- Key `ForegroundShortTracker` on `ownerHandle + totalSeconds` when no title is available, so the
  Short is tracked and *measured* regardless.
- Resolve identity at finalize from watch history, joined on **owner handle + duration** rather than
  title. Refuse when two candidates match, exactly as now.

That turns every remaining title-gate loss into a resolvable listen, and it removes the whole class
of regression rather than its fifth instance.

### 8.11 Identity fixed by removing the title from it

unreadable footer title no longer refuses the Short, because the title was never identity evidence —
it was a *selector*. The handle and the seekbar prove a Short is playing and give its exact length,

- `recentShortIds(title = null)` offers the most recent feed Shorts instead of selecting by title.

| `en-US` (what the resolver sends) | `#musica … #noticias` | `#music … #news` |
| `es-419` | `#musica … #noticias` | `#musica … #noticias` |

The **upload** is Spanish, and the *displayed* rendering is a property of the fetch rather than of
the video. The `localizedTitle` fallback was reached — that was the problem. It substituted the
displayed title whenever its **title key** equalled the frozen one, and a title made entirely of
hashtags has an **empty key**, so every such title matched vacuously. The guard therefore replaced
the Spanish name the screen had actually shown with the English rendering the resolver's own `en-US`
fetch produced, handed that to `VideoTitleMatcher` — which does *not* strip hashtags — and refused
the listen for the difference.

**Fix.** A page publishes two names for one id, and agreement with **either** is agreement.
`VideoIdentityCorroborator` no longer chooses one; it compares the frozen title against both and
takes the strongest evidence. Both names come from the one page being verified, so no new candidate
is admitted, and duration, owner handle and the unique-id rule are untouched. When neither agrees,
the refusal now prints both.

Verified on the device, on the exact video, end to end:

```
15:09:15  [history] resolved Short "#xbox … #musica … #noticias …" → RTQFqbCPUGg
15:09:15  [native-id] verified RTQFqbCPUGg via corroborated resolver (run-local verified candidate)
15:10:32  [engine] scrobbled (block): @tendencia_tv — … — tx 0d02673d8615cb911cdcfe0de145d055079aee00
```

The unit test reproduces the old refusal character-for-character against the 13:06 log line.

### 14.2 §13.6.2 zero-width spaces — checked, nothing to fix

`SearchResultsParser.titleKey` and `VideoTitleMatcher.tokens` both already normalize `U+200B`: it is
neither `\p{L}` nor `\p{N}`, so the `[^\p{L}\p{N}]+ → " "` pass collapses it, and `HASHTAG` carries
no word-boundary requirement, so a hashtag followed immediately by one still strips as a hashtag.
Proven by test rather than by reading — `zero-width spaces in a screen title are not identity
evidence` — so the next session does not have to wonder again. It was never a second cause.

### 14.3 §13.4 the two untitled Shorts — a different bug entirely

Not the same family as §13.3. Both were refused **instantly** (19 ms and 26 ms after finalize), with
no feed read and no page fetch, which rules corroboration out before any theory of it is needed:

```
14:33:00.366  [finalize] null — played 10s of 16s
14:33:00.385  [engine] skipped: … this Short's title could not be read and watch history could not
                                  identify it from its owner handle and length
```

`WatchHistoryHealth` had stood the route down. Three "misses" at 14:25:28, 14:25:39 and 14:28:01
armed a fifteen-minute pause — and **all three were the same track**, "See Every TIME Cover From
2025", looked up again each time the owner replayed it. The diagnosis that counter exists for is
"the YouTube app is on another account", which shows up as *different* tracks going missing, so one
track counted three times is not evidence of it. `recentShortIds` then returned an empty list
without logging anything at all, and the engine's refusal blamed sign-in.

**Two fixes.** A miss now carries the track it is about, and consecutive misses of the same track
count once; a stood-down route says so (`[history] not offering Shorts candidates: …`), and the
Short's refusal names the real state instead of pointing at sign-in.

Both Shorts were on the current build, so §13.4's premise was right about the path and wrong about
the gate.

### 14.4 §13.2 Iggy Azalea — cause established, and it is not the continuation timer

```
12:41:36  [session] − YouTube (session ended)      ← Doja Cat ended at 201s of 201s, scrobbled
          … 11 minutes 35 seconds with no MediaSession at all …
12:53:11  [session] + YouTube                      ← pos=94111ms, speed 2.0, of a 227s video
12:53:18  [session] − YouTube (session ended)      ← seven seconds later
12:54:18  [finalize] [session continuation expired] — played 12s of 227s
```

YouTube published a MediaSession for this video for **seven seconds**, ninety-four seconds into it,
and nothing before or after. Twelve seconds is a complete and accurate account of everything that
was ever published; the continuation timer did exactly what it should, and there is no measurement
to recover — first-sight position is not evidence of playback in *this* session, since a resumed
video opens mid-track having played nothing now, so crediting it would be inventing measurement.

What was wrong is that the line **read** like a fault. It now names the lead-in it could not have
seen: `played 12s of 227s (up to 2.0× speed), first seen 94s in — anything played before that was
never published to RustedWax`. Suppressed when progress was carried across a replacement session,
which already accounts for its own lead-in, and below a 10s floor.

The eleven-minute publication gap itself is unexplained, and is the real open question — see §14.6.

### 14.7 The MediaSession gap — one hypothesis measured and disproven

**Do not re-run.** "YouTube does not publish a MediaSession during ordinary foreground watch-page
playback" is false. Measured 2026-08-06 15:15, driving `_zR6ROjoOX0` from the Mac and polling
`adb shell dumpsys media_session` every 6s for a minute: YouTube's session was present in all ten
samples, `state=3` throughout, and RustedWax logged `[session] + com.google.android.youtube` within
a second of the video starting. The notification listener and the session callback are both fine in
this state.

So the 11m35s gap in §14.4 is a *state*, not the norm, and the next measurement has to catch it in
that state rather than in a clean one. The window ran from a video ending naturally at
`201s of 201s` through YouTube being on screen with the accessibility tree unreadable
(`captured 2 nodes`) — which is also the §5.1 signature, and the two may be one outage seen on two
channels.
